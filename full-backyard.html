Design type — full backyard

Full backyard design, drawn on the yard you already have.

A whole-yard concept is the only way to see how a terrace, lawn, planting and lighting behave as one place instead of four separate line items. PlotVisionAI™ renders the complete plan into a photograph of your property, so the layout is judged on your grade, your house and your tree line.

What a whole-yard concept settles

The arguments a full backyard plan has to win.

Whole-yard projects fail on relationships, not on materials. How far the terrace sits from the door, whether the lawn is still usable once the beds are in, where people walk when they are carrying something. PlotVisionAI is used by homeowners and landscape professionals worldwide, and it works on any property you can photograph — anywhere in the world.

Zones before finishes

A full-yard concept starts by dividing the property into things that get used: a hard surface for sitting and eating, an open piece of lawn, a boundary layer of planting, and the routes between them. Getting those proportions right on a picture of your own yard is worth more than choosing a paver.

Sight lines from inside the house

Most of the year, the yard is looked at rather than stood in. Generating the concept from the camera position of a kitchen or living-room window shows what the plan actually does to the view, which is the test a plan drawing never has to pass.

Sequencing and budget

Almost every whole-yard project gets built in stages. Seeing the finished intent first makes it possible to decide what phase one really is — usually the terrace and the grading — and what can wait a season without stranding the work already done.

One property, one frame

Every pair on this site holds the property still and changes only what is being decided: same house, same camera position, same light. That is the rule the tool is built around, because a concept that swaps in a nicer yard is not a concept, it is a brochure.

What a full backyard concept does not decide

A concept visualization is not construction documentation. It does not carry base depths, drainage design, soil bearing, structural detail for walls or footings, irrigation zoning, electrical loads or the local code that governs any of it. It also does not price your project. What it does is get everyone — both spouses, the designer and the contractor bidding it — looking at the same yard before money moves.

Related design types: a patio or terrace is usually the first phase of a full-yard plan, retaining walls handle the grade it sits on, planting and lawn do the softening, and night lighting is what keeps the whole thing usable after dark.
