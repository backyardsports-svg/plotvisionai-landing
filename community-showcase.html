Technique Spotlight

What experienced installers actually check first

High-level planning notes, not specifications. Requirements vary by site, soil, climate and local code, so treat these as the questions to ask rather than numbers to build from.

  • Grading and drainage first

    Water direction decides most of the layout. Before anything is drawn, we look at where water arrives, where it leaves, and how much fall is available. Concepts that ignore this look good and build badly. Local codes and engineering requirements vary, so treat this as a planning conversation, not a specification.

  • Patio base preparation

    The visible pavers are the easy part. Base depth, compaction in lifts, and edge restraint are what keep the surface flat years later. Depth depends on soil and use, which is why we talk about it on site rather than quoting a number here.

  • Wall construction and grade changes

    Retaining walls are structures. Height, backfill, drainage behind the wall and, above a certain height, engineering and permits all come into it. Visualization settles the look, material and terrace layout; the structural side is decided with the installer.

  • Planting composition

    Three layers read well from the house: canopy, mid-height mass, and low edge. Repeat a small number of species rather than collecting many. Concepts are most useful for checking bed shape and mass before plants are bought.

  • Low-voltage night lighting

    Aim before you add. Most schemes improve by moving fixtures and shielding glare rather than increasing count. Warm colour temperature keeps planting and stone looking natural, and the fixture itself should stay out of the sight line.

  • Fencing layout

    Height, sight lines and gate positions matter more than panel style. Walk the line, then test the height in a concept from the windows you actually look out of — screening that works at the property line often does nothing from inside the house.

  • Court surfacing and resurfacing

    Surface colour, line layout and containment height change how a court reads and plays. Resurfacing decisions usually start with the existing pad: its condition, drainage and orientation. Visualization is where colour and line combinations get settled before anyone commits.

What to include

  • One or two photos. A wide shot from a fixed position is worth more than ten close-ups.
  • Design type. Which of the eight it belongs to, so it lands in the right filter.
  • Your role. Homeowner or professional, and whether we may credit you.
  • What you were solving. The problem, not the product list.
  • Region, only if you want it shown. It can stay off the card entirely.

Review is manual and there is no queue position to check. We only publish with permission, and we leave out anything identifying: addresses, paperwork, plans with client details.
