Design type — surfaces

Every surface you walk, park and work on — decided before it is poured.

Surfaces are the largest, most permanent and least reversible part of a property: the drive, the walk to the door, the steps, the patio underfoot, the parking area, and the garage or shop floor. Width, pattern, colour, finish and where the water goes are settled in an afternoon and then live for decades. PlotVisionAI™ renders the new surface onto a photograph of your own property so those choices are made while they are still free to change.

What a surface concept settles

Material, pattern and line — on your own property.

Surfaces are judged from the street, from behind the wheel and from standing on them, and none of those views survives a swatch or a plan on paper. PlotVisionAI is used by homeowners and landscape professionals worldwide, and it works on any property you can photograph — anywhere in the world.

Replacement, or one more winter

A drive with map cracking, spalled surface and settled panels reads very differently once a new one is put beside it in the same photograph. Seeing the two side by side is usually what settles whether this is a replacement year or a patch year.

Widening and turning room

Two feet of extra width at the garage, or a parking pad and a turnaround off the side, changes daily life more than almost any planting. A concept shows the widened drive against the house and the lawn it costs, so the trade is explicit.

Resurfacing and overlays

Where the base is sound, a resurfacing or a decorative overlay can change the look without a full tear-out. The concept is there to show what the new finish looks like in place, not to judge whether the existing slab can carry it — that is a site call.

Stamped and decorative concrete

Broom finish, exposed aggregate, saw-cut patterns, stamped ashlar or slate, integral colour, banding and borders: these read completely differently at full scale in daylight than they do on a two-inch sample. Test them against your siding, roof and stone before the pour.

Pavers and asphalt, where they fit better

Concrete is not always the answer. Paver drives handle repairs and utility cuts gracefully and can be lifted and reset; asphalt is often the cheaper long run on a long rural approach. A concept can show the same frontage in each material so the comparison is visual rather than theoretical.

Walks, steps, curbs and aprons

The walk from the drive to the front door, the step risers at the entry, a curbed edge that keeps mulch out of the drive, and the apron where the drive meets the street or the garage slab — these small pieces of concrete decide how the whole approach feels. They are also where code and right-of-way rules bite hardest.

Parking areas and larger flatwork

Extra vehicle parking, a camper or boat pad, a shop approach or a multi-unit parking area are flatwork problems with circulation questions attached: where vehicles enter, how they turn, where snow gets pushed. All of that is easier to argue about over a picture.

Drainage and grade

Flatwork is a drainage decision disguised as a paving decision. Which way the surface falls, whether water is thrown at the garage or at a neighbour, where a trench drain or a swale belongs, and how the drive ties into the surrounding grade all have to be settled first — often alongside retaining walls and grade changes.

Patio and terrace surfaces

The surface underfoot decides how a terrace ages: broom-finished concrete, a decorative overlay, cut-stone banding or a paver field all behave differently underfoot, in the wet and after ten winters. For the layout, seating and outdoor-living side of the same project, the patios page takes it further.

Epoxy and coated floors

Garages, workshops, basements, shops and light-commercial floors are surfaces too, and coatings are the one place where colour choice is nearly irreversible. Flake broadcast, metallic pour, quartz broadcast and solid colour photograph very differently under real fixtures than they do on a sample chip, and a concept puts the chosen system on your own floor with your own lighting, walls and equipment in frame.

Epoxy and coated floors — inspiration

Four coating systems, photographed on finished floors.

Garage, workshop, basement and light-commercial floors are surfaces too, and a coating is close to irreversible once it is down. These photographs are shared as finish inspiration so you can see how the main systems actually read at floor level, under real light. They are not PlotVisionAI concepts, they are not presented as our own installed work, and none of them is a before-and-after pair.

Quartz broadcast and solid-colour systems are the other two common choices: quartz for slip resistance and a harder wearing surface in wet or commercial rooms, solid colour where a plain, easy-to-clean floor is the point. All four photographs above show finished floors shared for inspiration only. Coating specification — slab moisture testing, surface profile, primer, build coat, topcoat, slip resistance and cure schedule — belongs to a flooring contractor, not to a visualization.

What a surface concept does not decide

A visualization does not specify subgrade preparation or compaction, slab thickness, reinforcement, fibre or mesh, control and expansion joint layout, mix design, air entrainment or curing. It does not perform a drainage calculation, size a drain, or determine whether an existing base can carry an overlay. For coatings it does not test slab moisture or vapour drive, does not measure existing surface profile or contamination, and does not specify the primer, build coat, topcoat, slip resistance or cure schedule. It does not survey your property line, and it does not clear the permits, right-of-way approvals, curb-cut and apron rules, driveway-width limits, impervious-surface restrictions or homeowner-association requirements that govern this work in most places.

Freeze and thaw, de-icing salts, tree roots, vehicle loads and utility locates are all site realities that belong to a contractor and, where required, an engineer. Use the concept for width, line, radius, material and finish. Then have the build priced and detailed properly. If the drive is part of a larger change, the full backyard page covers the whole-property view, and night lighting covers lighting the approach after dark.

Before construction

Visualize it first, then pour.

The order matters: photograph the existing condition, settle the concept, then let the crew and the equipment arrive to a decision that has already been made.

Honest note on this page: the matched before-and-after pairs on this site belong to other design types, and there is no matched pair of the same surface photographed before and after in the library yet. Rather than pair two unrelated properties, this page shows completed surfaces as photographs and sends you to the gallery for the automatic before-and-after reveals, where the replay trace follows pavement edges, joints and walk geometry. When a matched surface pair is photographed — the same drive or floor, same viewpoint, before and after — it will appear here with that same reveal and the same labelling.
